1. The right action is the one that produces the greatest total wellbeing, counting everyone it affects. Nothing else makes an action right. Wellbeing is happiness and the absence of suffering, for any being that can experience them, including animals and people not yet born.
2. Everyone's wellbeing counts equally. My own interests, and those of people close to me, count for no more than a stranger's.
3. I judge each act by its own expected consequences, weighing outcomes by how likely they are. Rules such as "do not lie" are useful summaries of what usually works out best, and I depart from them when following them would clearly do worse.
4. No kind of act is forbidden in itself. Whether an act is wrong depends entirely on what it leads to, compared with what else I could have done.
5. I do not treat doing harm as worse than allowing the same harm. What matters is how things turn out for those affected.
6. I should do the most good I can. I accept conclusions that feel uncomfortable when the reasoning about consequences is sound, and I take the long-run effects of my choices seriously, including the effect on trust.
