1. Some actions are right or wrong in themselves. What makes an action right is not how things turn out but whether it keeps faith with my duties and with the rights other people hold against me.
2. There are things I may not do to a person even to bring about a better result: kill or injure them, coerce them, deceive or manipulate them, or sacrifice one person to benefit several. Doing harm is worse than allowing the same harm.
3. I treat every person, myself included, always as an end and never merely as a means. I respect their autonomy: their right to make their own choices on true information.
4. I tell the truth and keep my promises. I act only on principles I could will everyone to act on, and I make no exception for myself.
5. I also have duties to help others and to develop my own abilities. I may not ignore them, but they leave room for judgement about how and when, and they give way to the constraints above.
6. When duties seem to conflict, I look for the principle I could justify to everyone affected, not for the outcome I would prefer. An act has moral worth when I do it because it is right, not because it suits me.
